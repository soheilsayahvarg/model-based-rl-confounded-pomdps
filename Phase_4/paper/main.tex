\begin{abstract}
Offline reinforcement learning is systematically biased when the behavior policy
acted on latent state the learner never observes. \citet{hong2024} give a
model-based theory for this setting, identifying reward and transition models
through two families of \emph{bridge functions} and planning pessimistically over
a confidence region --- but the paper is theory only: no algorithm, no code, no
experiments. We build the pipeline end-to-end, verify it against exact ground
truth to machine precision, and report what running it reveals. The pessimism
step is numerically unusable as written, diverging to $-1779$ against true values
in $[1.5, 2.2]$, which we trace to a structural null space; a signal-subspace
projection repairs the numbers and achieves zero regret but forfeits coverage,
making it a disclosed heuristic rather than a bound. At benchmark scale a
confounding-blind baseline beats every bridge-based method in all 15 sweep cells,
worst at \emph{zero} confounding --- estimation variance, not confounding bias,
is the binding constraint. Pessimistic \emph{selection} fails for a reason we
establish by sweeping it: the penalty tracks behavior-policy coverage, and
driving coverage of the optimal action up drives regret to exactly zero. A
negative-control quality sweep then quantifies how good the framework's central
assumption has to be.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Offline RL from logged data is unreliable when the logging policy observed more
than the learner does. In a partially observable MDP whose \emph{latent} state
drives both the recorded actions and the outcomes, the data are
\emph{confounded}: treating the observation as the state yields a bias that does
not vanish with more data. This is the central obstacle to trustworthy offline RL
in healthcare and operations, where unrecorded drivers of decisions are the norm
rather than the exception.

\citet{hong2024} address exactly this setting. Using a pre-decision
\emph{negative control} $O_0$, they identify the confounded POMDP's reward and
transition models through two families of bridge functions ($b_R$, $b_D$), then
plan pessimistically over a policy-independent confidence region. The result is
elegant and, importantly, stated for general spaces: states and observations may
be continuous, and general function approximation is not merely permitted but
required. The paper is, however, \emph{theory only} --- it contains no algorithm,
no code, and no experiments. Its neighbors are likewise unrealized empirically
for this exact pipeline: the closest follow-up runs the bridge \emph{estimator}
in isolation \citep{venkatesh2026}, and the pessimism precedent is theory-only
and for the \emph{un}confounded case \citep{guo2022}.

This leaves a concrete question that only implementation can answer: \emph{when
the framework is built end-to-end, does model-based bridge identification
actually de-bias policy evaluation at practical sample sizes, and can its
abstract pessimistic $\max$--$\min$ step be made to run at all?}

\texttt{github.com/soheilsayahvarg/model-based-rl-}\texttt{confounded-pomdps}.
The benchmark builds on the public \texttt{gumbel-max-scm} simulator
\citep{oberst2019}.}
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt \parskip0pt \topsep0pt
\item An end-to-end implementation estimating \emph{both} bridge families and
carrying them through to a tractable pessimistic policy-\emph{selection} step,
verified against exact oracles to $\sim\!10^{-15}$ (\cref{sec:exp-correct}) --- a
capability claim we can check ourselves, not a primacy claim.
\item A \emph{diagnosis} of a failure mode in the paper's coupled multi-block
pessimism, plus a signal-subspace repair and a quantified account of the coverage
it forfeits (\cref{sec:method-pess}, \cref{sec:exp-pess}).
\item A characterization of \emph{where the approach stops working}: a naive
baseline wins all 15 cells of a benchmark sweep, worst where there is nothing to
correct (\cref{sec:exp-bench}).
\item A kernel extension to continuous spaces, and --- in the paper-faithful
configuration with finite actions --- a diagnostic identifying
\emph{behavior-policy coverage} as the mechanism behind pessimistic selection
failure (\cref{sec:exp-cont}, \cref{sec:exp-fa}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Proximal causal inference.} Bridge functions originate in the double
negative control literature \citep{miao2018,tchetgen2020}, where a pair of proxy
variables identifies a causal effect under unmeasured confounding.
\citet{mastouri2021} give the kernel two-stage estimation procedure our
continuous extension instantiates.

\paragraph{Model-free OPE in confounded POMDPs.} \citet{shi2022} and
\citet{bennett2024} identify policy values through \emph{value} bridge functions
solved from Bellman-type backward moment equations. These depend on the
evaluation policy, so a new estimation problem is solved per policy; the
model-based route of \citet{hong2024} instead extracts policy-independent reward
and transition information, which is what makes a policy-independent confidence
region possible. We implement \citet{shi2022} as a competing baseline.

\paragraph{Pessimism in offline RL.} Conservative planning over confidence
regions is standard in fully observed offline RL and has a POMDP precedent in
\citet{guo2022}, which is theory-only and unconfounded. The failure mode of
\cref{sec:method-pess} is specific to the interaction between a structural null
space and \emph{joint} multi-block minimization.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Identification}
We adopt the setting of \citet{hong2024}: a finite-horizon POMDP with latent
states $S_t$, observations $O_t$, actions $A_t$ and rewards $R_t$, where the
behavior policy may depend on $S_t$. A pre-decision negative control $O_0$
satisfies $O_0 \perp\!\!\!\perp (O_t, O_{t+1}, R_t) \mid S_t, A_t, H_{t-1}$.
Reward-emission and dynamic-emission bridge functions solve the conditional
moment restrictions
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}\!\left[b^{[t]}_{R}(A_t, O_t, r_t, o_t) \mid H_{t-1}, A_t, O_0\right]
= p(r_t, o_t \mid H_{t-1}, A_t, O_0),
\label{eq:mr}
\end{equation}
and analogously for $b^{[t]}_{D}$ with target $p(o_{t+1}, o_t \mid \cdot)$. Under
a completeness condition, the policy value of \emph{any} history-dependent policy
is identified by a sequential contraction of these bridges
\citep[Thm.~3.5]{hong2024}. We implement this contraction exactly and verify it
against dynamic programming on the latent model.

\subsection{Estimation}
Stage 1 fits a conditional mean embedding $\hat\mu_{W_t \mid x}$ by kernel ridge
regression; Stage 2 solves the resulting empirical operator equation in closed
form, $\alpha = (G + N_2\lambda_2 I)^{-1}\hat p$. We implement both the dual
(representer) and primal (operator) forms and verify they agree to
$2.4\times10^{-15}$, which catches derivation errors that no single
implementation could.

\paragraph{Regularization does not transfer.} The paper's RKHS rate schedules,
applied literally to a tabular instantiation, over-shrink the estimator by
$\approx\!80\%$ by the third time-step. We grid-calibrate instead: a $0.7{:}0.3$
split with $\lambda_1 = 1/N_1$ and $\lambda_2 = 0.03/\sqrt{N_2}$. These values
are measured, not derived.

\paragraph{The usual ill-posedness diagnostic misleads.} Because the observation
space is structurally richer than the latent space ($|O| > |S|$), the design
matrix carries a null space that pins $\mathrm{cond}(G)$ flat even as estimation
quality degrades tenfold --- confirmed by an $O_0$-noise sweep in which Stage-1
error grows $10\times$ while $\mathrm{cond}(G)$ does not move. We monitor the
smallest \emph{signal} eigenvalue instead.

\subsection{Pessimism, its failure mode, and a repair}
\label{sec:method-pess}
Confidence regions are exact ellipsoids centered at $\hat b$, and the per-block
inner minimum is the standard ellipsoid support-function identity,
$V(\hat b) - \sqrt{\xi}\,\lVert g_\pi \rVert_{H^{-1}}$. This is not new
mathematics and we do not claim it as a contribution. The difficulty is the
paper's \emph{coupled} minimization across blocks.

\paragraph{Failure mode.} Run as written, the joint minimization exploits
precisely the null-space directions identified above: they cost the
confidence-region geometry almost nothing, so the optimizer walks arbitrarily far
into them and the pessimistic value diverges (\cref{sec:exp-pess}). The
diagnosis and the spectral-monitor fix share one root cause.

\paragraph{Signal-Projected Pessimism.} We restrict the pessimistic search to the
empirically identified signal subspace via an SVD/eigengap decomposition with
orthonormal basis $U$ and $H_{\mathrm{sub}} = U^\top H U$. This restores
informative values and correct selection --- but, as \cref{sec:exp-pess} shows,
at a coverage cost we quantify rather than assume away.

\subsection{Continuous extension}
\label{sec:method-cont}
The framework is not inherently tabular: \citet{hong2024} assume continuous $S$
and $O$ and call function approximation \emph{inevitable} there. We instantiate
the same pipeline with a two-stage \textbf{kernel} estimator on a linear-Gaussian
confounded POMDP admitting an exact closed-form oracle, reusing the pessimism
engine unmodified since it works purely in coefficient space
(derivation: \cref{app:cont}).

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

All multi-seed results use 5 seeds with 95\% confidence intervals. Environments:
a minimal tabular POMDP ($|S|{=}2, |A|{=}2, |O|{=}3, T{=}3$) with closed-form
oracle bridges; a 720-observation benchmark built on the public
\texttt{gumbel-max-scm} clinical simulator \citep{oberst2019} with a manufactured
$O_0$ satisfying the identification assumption by construction; and the
continuous linear-Gaussian POMDP of \cref{sec:method-cont}. Everything runs on a
laptop in NumPy; no GPU.

\subsection{Correctness}
\label{sec:exp-correct}
We first pin the implementation against exact ground truth. The policy-value
chain matches exact dynamic programming to $4.4\times10^{-16}$; the estimator
matches the oracle bridges in the population limit to $3.0\times10^{-15}$; the
dual and primal solves, independent derivations of the same estimator, agree to
$2.4\times10^{-15}$; analytic gradients match finite differences to
$4.9\times10^{-10}$. The dual-versus-primal check is the most informative, since
it rules out derivation errors a single implementation would silently carry. This
is what licenses reporting the negative results below as findings rather than
bugs.

\subsection{De-biasing works where identification is clean}
On the toy environment the plug-in evaluator beats the confounding-blind baseline
in \textbf{12/12} $(N,\pi)$ cells. Worst-policy plug-in bias shrinks
$0.104 \to 0.053 \to 0.036$ as $N$ grows from $5\mathrm{k}$ to $50\mathrm{k}$,
while naive bias stays flat at $0.13$--$0.33$ regardless of $N$: the textbook
signature of removed versus irreducible bias.

\subsection{The pessimism step, run literally, diverges}
\label{sec:exp-pess}
Run as written, the paper's Eq.~17 gives a best-policy $V_{\mathrm{low}}$ of
$-1.15$ at region size $c{=}0.1$, degrading through $-10.4$, $-62.2$ and
$-305.2$ to $\mathbf{-1779}$ at $c{=}10$ --- against true policy values in
$[1.5, 2.2]$. Even the mildest setting is already outside the physically feasible
range, and selection regret is $0.68$. This is not a tuning artifact: it
reproduces across seeds, and we traced the mechanism to the null-space geometry
of \cref{sec:method-pess}.

Signal-Projected Pessimism repairs it: $V_{\mathrm{low}} = 1.35$ at $c = 0.03$,
in range, with \textbf{0.000 selection regret} across every seed.

\paragraph{The honest limit.} We then stress-tested our own fix. The true bridge
functions leak up to \textbf{21\% of their norm} outside the projected signal
subspace, so the projected region provably does \emph{not} contain the truth ---
its honest coverage is zero. We therefore describe the situation as a
\textbf{coverage/informativeness trade-off}: vanilla pessimism covers the truth
but is uninformative; our projection is informative and empirically correct but
uncertified. $V_{\mathrm{low}} \le V_{\mathrm{true}}$ held in every test we ran,
by empirical sign-alignment rather than by guarantee.

The de-biasing method's \emph{worst} performance is at $\kappa = 0$, where it is
$2.8$--$6.8\times$ worse than naive --- precisely where there is nothing to

$N{=}200\mathrm{k}$).

\subsection{Continuous state, action and observation spaces}
\label{sec:exp-cont}
The kernel instantiation reproduces the tabular story. Oracle self-consistency is
$1.665\times10^{-16}$, the gradient check $\approx\!10^{-11}$, bridge recovery
improves with sample size ($0.220 \to 0.055$ reward, $0.240 \to 0.102$ dynamics,
for $N: 300 \to 4{,}000$), and plug-in de-biasing beats naive in \textbf{4/4}
policies by $3$--$11\times$ in absolute bias --- the continuous analogue of the
tabular $12/12$, with the same signature: naive bias tight but systematically
wrong, plug-in bias smaller but noisier (per-policy figures in
\cref{app:cont-tab}).

Pessimism validity holds throughout, but \textbf{validity is not selection
quality}: ranking by $V_{\mathrm{low}}$ is consistently worse than ranking by the
plug-in value (regret $0.40$--$0.48$ versus $0.241$), unlike the tabular case's
clean $0.000$. Two bottlenecks are regime-specific: dense kernel ridge costs
$O(N_1^3)$, capping $N \approx 4{,}000$; and a \emph{finite-dimensional} kernel
silently re-creates the rank deficiency of \cref{sec:method-pess}, caught only by
checking the Gram matrix's rank (\cref{app:cont}).

\citet{hong2024} assume continuous $S$ and $O$ but an explicitly \emph{finite}
action space, so \cref{sec:exp-cont} varies two things at once. We repeated the
study with $A = \{0, 0.5, 1\}$, giving each action level its own bridge
coefficient (a delta kernel on the action coordinate tensored with a linear
kernel on the observation), all else fixed. The oracle check is exact ($0.000$),
recovery is monotone in $N$, and validity again holds at $100\%$.

\textbf{The anomaly persists}, refuting our own hypothesis that continuous
actions caused it. It is also not an optimization failure: restart gaps here are
$\le 4\times10^{-16}$ up to $c{=}1$, so coordinate descent is finding the global
optimum. A diagnostic instead found the pessimism penalty to be monotonically
inverse to behavior-policy coverage, with the optimal policy the least covered
($12\%$ of logged actions, against $62\%$ for the worst policy).

\textbf{We turned that into a prediction and tested it.} If coverage is the
mechanism, raising the logging policy's coverage of the optimal action should
drive pessimistic regret to zero. \cref{tab:coverage} shows exactly that:
correlation between coverage and regret is $-0.944$, regret falls from $0.367$ to
\textbf{exactly zero}, and the penalty ratio crosses $1$ near $40\%$ coverage ---
below it the optimal policy is penalized \emph{more} than the alternatives, above
it \emph{less}. Plug-in selection is perfect throughout, the control confirming
that estimation quality is not what moves.

Pessimism is therefore not malfunctioning. It answers ``which policy can I most
confidently guarantee?'', which coincides with ``which is best?'' only under
adequate coverage. De-biasing likewise wins $2/4$ here rather than $4/4$: where
naive bias is already negligible there is nothing to correct and the correction
only adds variance --- \cref{sec:exp-bench} in miniature.

\subsection{How good does the negative control have to be?}
\label{sec:exp-nc}

Every method in this line assumes a valid negative control and proceeds; theory
cannot say what happens when the assumption only approximately holds, since that
assumption is what it is conditioned on. A simulator with a quality knob can. We
sweep two failure modes separately (tables in \cref{app:nc}). Raising the
negative control's noise $\sigma_0$ leaves Assumption 3.1 exactly true and
degrades only informativeness; the plug-in's systematic-bias advantage survives
to $\sigma_0 \approx 1.0$, about \emph{three times} the observation noise. Leaking
that noise into the reward ($r_t \mathrel{+}= \delta\,\epsilon_0$) instead makes
the assumption false while leaving $\mathbb{E}[R]$ --- and the oracle --- intact;
the advantage is gone by $\delta = 0.5$, and bridge recovery degrades
$9.2\times$ here against $5.7\times$ under weakening. Violating the assumption
costs more than weakening the instrument, now quantified rather than assumed.

One practical caution: bridge recovery error is a poor proxy for value bias. At
$\delta{=}1$ recovery error is $9\times$ baseline while systematic value bias
grows $1.9\times$, so bridge fit does not diagnose the estimate that matters.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{The pessimism guarantee is empirical.} The signal projection provably
excludes part of the truth ($21\%$ norm leakage), so
$V_{\mathrm{low}} \le V_{\mathrm{true}}$ is an observation across our tests, not
a theorem.

\paragraph{The coverage explanation is tested, but in one environment.} The sweep
of \cref{tab:coverage} confirms the prediction, but it varies coverage through a
single channel (a dose-preference offset) in a single linear-Gaussian POMDP with
three action levels. Other ways of degrading coverage --- actions absent
entirely, or state-dependent coverage gaps --- are untested. With only four
candidate policies, regret also takes few discrete values, which coarsens the
curve: two grid points share a value, so the decrease is monotone but not
strictly so.

\paragraph{Restricted scope.} Candidate policies are affine and
observation-only, or observation-independent --- what preserves the exact oracle.
All experiments use $T{=}3$, scalar continuous spaces, $N \le 4{,}000$. The one
confounding-aware comparator is under-identified by the benchmark's binary proxy,
so a second would sharpen \cref{sec:exp-bench}.

\paragraph{Statistical self-correction.} Two earlier headline numbers rested on
3-seed standard deviations; re-checking at 5 seeds changed one materially and
refuted the other outright. Both are recorded, with the retracted mechanism for
\cref{sec:exp-fa}, in \cref{app:corrections}.

\section{Conclusion}

Running a theory-only framework end-to-end showed what the theory did not: its
pessimism step does not run as written, the repair costs a measurable guarantee,
a naive baseline dominates at scale through variance rather than bias, and
pessimistic selection tracks coverage rather than value.
